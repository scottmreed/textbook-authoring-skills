## Full evidence

### Independent persona reports

#### Organic Chemistry Instructor — 7.4/10

Go, with revisions — I would assign the prose as written, but not the assessment set or figure set as written. The chemistry is sound: every one of the 20 asset SMILES and every structure in the 48 questions parses in RDKit with the correct molecular formula (arenium ion C6H6Br+, Meisenheimer complex C6H4ClN2O5-, 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene, m-bromonitrobenzene and p-nitrotoluene regiochemistry all verified), and I could not find a wrong answer key: reactivity rankings, director classifications, hotspot atom indices (0-indexed, consistent across all four hotspots), the pi-electron counts (4 for the arenium cation, 6 for the Meisenheimer anion), resonance-form counts (3 generic, 4 for anisole), the xylene isomer counts (1 for para, 2 for ortho), both bond-change ledgers, and both synthesis routes are all correct. Prerequisite slugs all resolve to concepts in earlier packages, and the compiled question set is byte-identical in prompt/key/config to the source. Three things hold it back from instructor-ready. First, the chapter teaches its two hardest ideas — the arenium-ion resonance comparison that generates ortho/para vs meta, and every mechanism in the chapter — entirely in prose: there is not one curved-arrow figure, not one side-by-side resonance comparison, and the single video brief is deferred with an empty URL and a hidden reader block. Second, assessment is lopsided: 11 of 24 unique items sit on the mechanism and directing-effect concepts, while the advanced nucleophilic-aromatic-substitution/benzyne concept gets only classification-level items and side-chain chemistry gets one, leaving several stated objectives (draw the Meisenheimer complex, the 14C benzyne evidence, NBS benzylic bromination, nitro-to-amine reduction) unassessed. Third, two framing choices will have to be un-taught: free 'Br+' as a reactant in the energy profile and question prompts, contradicting the chapter's own insistence that Br2 is not electrophilic enough without FeBr3; and the assertion that direct alkylation with 1-chloropropane 'passes through a free alkyl carbocation.' The chapter also never warns that aniline cannot be nitrated directly, even though it trains students to classify -NH2 as a strong ortho-para activator.

**Strengths:**

- Answer keys hold up under checking: reactivity rankings (phenol > toluene > benzene > chlorobenzene > nitrobenzene; anisole > benzene > bromobenzene > benzaldehyde), director classifications including F and I as deactivating ortho–para directors, both hotspot keys under a consistent 0-indexed atom convention, 4 vs 6 pi electrons for the cationic vs anionic cyclohexadienyl intermediates, 3 vs 4 resonance forms for benzene vs anisole, 1 vs 2 xylene mononitration isomers, and both bond-change ledgers including the formal-charge bookkeeping on the base.
- The rate-versus-orientation distinction — the single hardest idea in the chapter — is taught explicitly ('rate and orientation are answers to two different questions'), reinforced in the halogen discussion, and then assessed directly by an error_repair item whose diagnosis category is literally 'Error conflating rate with orientation'.
- Assessment breadth is unusually good: 24 unique items across 15 question types, including structure-drawing (structure_scaffold with an RDKit engine), mechanism bookkeeping, energy-profile reading, synthesis routing, and structured claim–evidence–reasoning, each with a paired variant.
- Feedback bundles teach rather than score: targeted wrong-answer explanations (for example, why selecting -Cl in the activator item is 'the classic trap') plus three-level hint ladders that move from a general question to a specific comparison without giving the answer away.
- Every prerequisite slug resolves to a real concept in an earlier topic package (benzene-structure-and-stability, electrophilic-addition-carbocation, sn1-reactivity-factors, substitution-and-elimination-overview, allylic-bromination), and the compiled question set matches the source package exactly on prompt_text, answer_key, student_config, concept_slug, and difficulty for all 48 items.

**Findings (18; blockers: none):**

- **instr-001** [high/misconception] (asset_id=rc-eas-bromination-profile, question_slug=ch16-arenium-sp3-carbon, nugget_id=nugget-eas-mechanism; confidence 0.93)
 - Observation: Free Br+ is used as a discrete reactant in three student-facing places — the reaction-coordinate asset's minima_labels ('Benzene + Br⁺' ... 'Bromobenzene + H⁺'), the hotspot prompt ('the arenium ion formed when benzene reacts with Br⁺'), and the nugget practice_check ('the arenium ion formed when benzene attacks Br⁺') — while the prose two paragraphs above states the opposite: 'Bromine and chlorine are not electrophilic enough to react with benzene at a useful rate, so a Lewis acid catalyst ... polarizes the X–X bond.' The matching question handles this correctly ('Br⁺ delivered from the Br₂–FeBr₃ complex'), so the chapter is internally inconsistent about whether a free bromenium ion exists. The same profile also drops HBr/FeBr4- and shows 'H⁺' as the leaving fragment, which the prose explicitly attributes to FeBr4- acting as the base.
 - Learner impact: Students who read the figure and answer the questions will write Br+ and H+ as free species in mechanisms and on exams — precisely the answer most instructors mark wrong — and will lose the causal link between the Lewis acid catalyst and the reaction happening at all, which is one of the chapter's stated trouble spots.
 - Evidence: asset rc-eas-bromination-profile spec.minima_labels = ['Benzene + Br⁺','Arenium ion','Bromobenzene + H⁺']; ch16-arenium-sp3-carbon prompt_text; nugget-eas-mechanism practice_check prompt; contrasted with nugget-nitration-sulfonation-halogenation expanded text and ch16-electrophile-matching option r3.
 - Need: The chapter needs one consistent convention for the halogenation electrophile and for the proton acceptor across prose, figure labels, practice checks, and question prompts — the electrophile shown as delivered from the Br2–FeBr3 complex and the second step shown giving HBr with regenerated catalyst, rather than free ions.
- **instr-002** [high/chemical-accuracy] (question_slug=ch16-alkylation-vs-acylation-v2, nugget_id=nugget-friedel-crafts; confidence 0.82)
 - Observation: The comparison-matrix key marks the feature 'Passes through a free alkyl carbocation' as 'yes' for direct alkylation with 1-chloropropane and AlCl3, and the expanded prose asserts 'the free carbocation intermediate' and 'the cation from 1-chlorobutane shifts a hydride to become the secondary butyl cation before attacking the ring.' Primary alkyl halides do not ionize to free primary carbocations under Friedel–Crafts conditions; they react through a polarized R–Cl···AlCl3 complex in which the hydride shift is essentially concurrent with ionization. As written, the graded answer requires students to affirm a species that does not exist.
 - Learner impact: Students are graded on a statement they will be told is wrong in the next course, and they lose the more useful idea — that rearrangement tracks how much carbocation character develops — which also explains why methylation and tert-butylation behave so differently.
 - Evidence: ch16-alkylation-vs-acylation-v2 answer_key [redacted]; nugget-friedel-crafts text.expanded, 'Fourth, the free carbocation intermediate is subject to the same hydride and alkyl shifts encountered in SN1 chemistry'.
 - Need: The chapter needs the primary-halide case stated at the accuracy level it will be held to later — cation-like character in a halide–Lewis acid complex rather than a free primary cation — and the graded feature reworded so the correct answer is defensible.
